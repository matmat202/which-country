Which Country? · a story

One number

The daily game Which Country? shows two countries and asks which is higher: who lives longer, who is richer, whose population is older. Eight questions, countries.

Here is a shortcut that knows one thing per country: how many children a woman there has, on average. It always picks the country with fewer.

Each dot is a country. Left to right, children per woman, from in to in . Up the side, its rank in life expectancy. The line falls the whole way: fewer children, longer lives.

Chapter 3 · Towns and cities

The weakest link

Which is more urban? The line still falls, but the cloud is wide. Some countries with few children are rural, some with many are mostly cities. Right of the time, the shortcut's worst.

Chapter 4 · Population

The one it cannot do

Which has more people? Children per woman says nothing about it. India and China, the two biggest, have fewer children than average. Nigeria and Pakistan have more. So on this question the shortcut flips a coin.

Chapter 5 · The score

out of 10

Over years of the game's daily tens, the shortcut averages right a day. On "where do women have more children?" it knows the answer. On population it guesses. On the other six it is right of the time.

Every round the game will serve from its first day for days: rounds where the shortcut is the whole answer.

Chapter 6 · Round by round

Until the countries get close

The game starts with two countries worlds apart and ends with two neck and neck. The shortcut fades round by round: right of the time in round 1, in round 5, and in round 10, barely better than a coin.

That is what the last rounds are for. A broad sense of which countries are rich and which are poor gets you through the start. At the end you have to know the numbers.

Coda · Play it

Ten a day

This page measures that the shortcut works, not why. Falling birth rates, longer lives, older populations and higher incomes tend to arrive together, and one number happens to stand in for all of them.

Which Country? asks ten a day. Now you know how to get through the first few.
